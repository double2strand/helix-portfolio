Nature's Dirge: An Oak's Hymn

Tucked into crevices laden with soil and weed,

The tale I now tell, began with me as a seed.

Solitary I slept amidst nature’s earthen dough,

Not witness to the land’s dip from field to fallow.

‘Twixt the seasons my slumber slept on,

Conscious was my mind without knowledge of its dawn.

I laboured in my dreams to make the earth hospitable,

Ignorant to the malice above that had made the planet despicable.

In the searing light of an unfortunate noon,

My slumber ended like an untimely boon.

This is when I reared above the surface of the earth,

Only to note that there hadn’t ever been a more macabre birth.

Visions had held aloft my tiring tenure of sleep,

Portraits of colour with green tresses and valleys deep.

My first sight of earth an infernal contrast now bore,

The cracked terrain I now stood upon was a tale of untold gore.

What devilish ghoul had borne upon it such disastrous calamity?

What infernal wraith had cast me into this picture of depravity?

As I now stood upon this land as barren as death,

I sought eagerly a reason to draw every next breath.

My penance for birth into beauty had been cast as futile,

And yet I continued to rise and grow on this terrain utterly vile.

Parched, I gazed at the heavens above in plea,

Whilst the waters of oblivion washed over me in vociferous glee.

In solitude was I borne into this infernal land’s womb,

And in solitude shall I dwell till I delve into my tomb.

Nature hadn’t been the divinity it had promised to be,

And yet I chose to stand alone in this barren desolate sea.

*** ***  ***

Time had flown past me like a swift bird of prey,

Each time casting me into darker shades of grey.

I now stood tall in all my forlorn majesty,

Over the remains of my creator’s mutilated travesty.

A task of seemingly trivial consequence I had begun in my slumber,

With years and years of solitude to encumber.

Although my birth had been into all but nature’s royalty,

I still had been nurtured with every ounce of the soil’s fledgling loyalty.

I had sworn to give the earth every ounce of my own,

For a little ray of hope my mind had in me sown.

All that I bore – be it fruit, leaf or wood,

Was evidence of the belief that with me stood.

Visions of pretext had marked my beginning on earth,

And yet in nurturing me the fallow soil showed no dearth.

My solitude, my pain was a panegyric of earth’s resolve,

My age and my life, an ode to its efforts to old sins, absolve.

***  ***  ***

In the searing light of a fortunate noon,

An axe struck my chest with the thud of a gracious boon.

But as I fell and lay asunder, my dying eyes met a sight of grace,

Saplings erupting from the earth’s previously scarred face.

My birth had been caused amidst utter disharmony,

But death now brought with it a strange familiar symphony.

Solitude in birth and solitude in a life of strife,

And yet in death my visions stood true – an earth where nature’s beauty was rife.
