The Churning of the Milky Ocean : A Great Indian Chronicle

In the stunning, utopian halls of pearl,

Watching merrily, the grace of many a dainty girl;

Was seated the Lord of the Kings of Kings.

Indra, the great; ornated in pearls and rings.

And in the midst of all this glory,

We have a sudden twist in the story.

The tranquil and sublime Durvasa then came;

He was the ascetic who attained Elysian fame.

He had been yonder to the incorporeal mountains,

Where he was amazed by Sri Vishnu’s eternal fountains.

To Indra, a flower garland he gave.

But the mighty king was in a mood too naïve.

Indra flung the holy garland around the neck of an elephant.

And thus Durvasa was flared, in a rage; both obstreperous and turbulent.

He cursed Indra and the three worlds, he ruled;

That they would forever, forever be doomed.

The devils and demons went on a rout of murder and plunder;

Destruction and terror was all that they could render.

The gods had to face a gargantuan defeat.

Whilst the earth was swept by, in diabolical deceit.

To the feet of Vishnu flocked the entire Elysium,

And they begged and pleaded to him to help them.

And thus with his guidance, a strategy was devised;

To avenge the deaths of all the gods who had demised.

“The demons are a cogent force”, Sri Vishnu gently said.

“So, not war my friends, but peace instead”

The Lord’s crafty smile portrayed it all;

That his prudent vision would never fall.

And then a great set of events were in motion,

According to Vishnu’s percipient notion.

The fruits of the event held a seduction so great,

That the avaricious demons never let go of their bait.

And upon the greatest cerulean lawns,

Came the benighted demons with their distinct horns;

They were led by Bali, of a mighty stature.

But; wicked, cruel and callous in nature.

The sublime gods then gently alighted,

Seeing their aura, all humans delighted.

They were led by Vishnu, the greatest of gods.

And with him came many invincible lords.

Mandara, the greatest of mountains was uprooted;

And, to the depths of the azure, it was fast rooted.

Vasuki, the mightiest of the serpents was called;

And his body, around the giant mount, was revolved.

The demons, sharpishly, caught hold of the head,

Whilst the gods had to tug on the hind instead.

This was a giant churning, and the mountain was the rod;

The rope was the serpent which had devoured many a cod.

And then as the magnificent churning continued,

There emerged a deadly poison, which smoked and fumed.

A drop of it could destroy the earth;

It hindered all processes of life and its birth.

Lord Shiva then emerged to initiate an abation,

Of this malaise of such a high degree of abomination.

He swallowed the poison; but his consort demure;

Was filled with fear and thus, she clutched his throat to turn it azure.

Then emerged many articles holy and pure,

From the fathoms of the deep abyss azure.

The gods and the demons churned on and on,

Till they had no sense of dusk or dawn.

Amidst the churning, the wedding bells rang,

At the arrival of Lakshmi, the gods all sang.

She placed a crown of lotuses on Vishnu’s head,

And he in turn placed her in his heart on an eternal golden thread.

Then the designed plot of the gods came to grace;

As the elixir of life was met with an obstreperous embrace.

It went to the demons and they fought for its possession

But Vishnu’s maiden incarnation filled the demons with too strong a temptation.

She was Mohini, a beauteous maiden,

She was a feminine Vishnu, with pulchritude laden.

Craftily seducing the demons to the madness of lust.

She gifted the goblet of amrit to the gods as she must.

As the demons woke from their slumber of avarice,

Realized their failure and raged in spite and malice.

But was it of any use?

It was obviously; destined for them to lose.
