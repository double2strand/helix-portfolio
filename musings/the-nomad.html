7 August 2025 | Poetry, Romance

The Nomad

Wound by the yarn of the winter wool, she gazed

At the forlorn crypt.

Engraved in black, in a slender script,

Was the tale of the nomad she had loved:-

‘I have no God, I have no laws’

‘And I do not believe in the lines that destiny draws’

A solitary tear tumbled into her robes,

Though strong she was, and pacified in soul,

And as her heart wailed like a devilish ghoul,

She sang out to the night; the tale of the nomad she had loved……..

Born in a village, in the yonder mountains,

He was a child none had yet perceived,

His tempestuous grey eyes could have the devil; deceived.

Such was the nomad she had loved ……….

Like a wild, alpine wind, of intense beauty.

He grew in strength and manly splendour,

His mane untamed was of seductive grandeur.

Such was the nomad she had loved ………

To ken the world was his deepest desire.

He culled education with his insidious glance,

And left the wise-heads of the village in a devastated trance.

Such was the nomad she had loved ………

His parents had loved him deeply, and yet;

He broke their hearts, as he moved along,

To dwell in the bliss of his heart’s melancholy song.

Such was the nomad she had loved ………

He strode beneath the star-tainted skies, and

Quelled heartily, the god above, and

Defied the might of every monarch, like water denied by sand.

Such was the nomad she had loved ………..

He knew not, the infernal flames of love, but

Was caught in the pyre of crucifying love,

By an angelic lass, descended from heaven above.

Such was the nomad she had loved ………

She had gazed; but once into his solemn eyes,

Enraptured by the cold, grey, tempestuous din,

She drunk from his eyes, an intoxicating gin.

Such was the nomad she had loved ………

He showed not his deadly craving for her; yet

She knew well his heart’s greatest delight,

And so, she loved him till the morning light.

Such was the nomad she had loved …….

An inscription, he had left beside her, on the tender grass:-

‘I have no god, I have no laws’

‘And I do not believe in the lines that destiny draws’

In blood unmistakably, his own;

He had no god, he had no laws….but she…..She was his own.

Such was the nomad she had loved…….

He did not trust the bondage of emotions,

He heeded no logic that man had constructed,

He fielded no burdens that god had obstructed.

Such was the nomad she had loved ……

He knew his task, he knew his doom;

From the vain, he stole their diabolic wealth,

And the famished, he nurtured back to health.

Such was the nomad she had loved ……

He battled inconspicuously against all malice.

He dealt death blows to those of a violent creed,

And with despise, tricked those of an aristocratic breed.

Such was the nomad she had loved …….

But then, while yet in the prime of his youth;

Caught unawares, he was, behind the wrought iron of slavery.

‘Despicable autocrat’ they termed him for his baneful bravery.

Such was the nomad she had loved …….

He had no law, he had no god; and so they said, he would have no trial.

‘As the sun of morn shall uprise, nomad, thou shall be charred to death’

‘Upon the flames of royal decree, until thou draw thine last breath.’

Such was the nomad she had loved ……..

And, yet as she yearned his touch and scent;

He was dealt his death in this callous fashion,

And she felt her hopes meekly flutter and, ashen,

Such was the nomad she had loved …….

Such was her love, such was her desire;

She had no god, she had no law;

She did not believe in the lines that destiny would draw.

Such was the lass who loved the nomad …….

And now, she stood afore his crypt;

The silent mist; a death-knell struck, and

She fell to her knees; clasped hand to hand.

Such was the lass who loved the nomad ……

They had no god, they had no laws;

They did not believe in the lines that destiny draws.

Such was the nomad she had loved …….

And; such was the lass who loved the nomad.
